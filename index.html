Local Knowledge

Common Causes of Water Damage in Plano Homes & Businesses

Plano sits on Blackland Prairie expansive clay soil that swells in rain and shrinks during drought. That seasonal movement stresses pipes, foundations and plumbing connections across neighborhoods from Willow Bend to Haggard Park. Knowing the cause tells us exactly where the water traveled and how each material needs to be dried.

Slab Leaks from Expansive Clay Soil

Blackland Prairie clay shifts under Plano foundations constantly. That movement cracks or separates copper and PEX water lines running under concrete slabs, especially common in older Willow Bend, Kings Ridge and Preston Meadow homes built on post-tension slabs. Warm spots on tile, damp baseboards with no visible leak, or a sudden spike in your water bill are the typical signs. Your plumber repairs the pipe; we dry the slab, subfloor, flooring and wall cavities around it. More on slab leak water damage in Plano.

Attic Pipe Bursts & Second-Floor Appliance Overflows

Many Plano homes in Deerfield, Hunters Glen, Canyon Creek and Chase Oaks have water heaters, HVAC condensate lines and plumbing in the attic, plus laundry rooms on the second floor. When a supply line bursts, a condensate pan overflows, or a washing machine hose fails, the water travels down through attic insulation, ceiling drywall and wall cavities before anyone notices. See burst pipe water damage, appliance leak damage, and water heater and AC leaks.

North Texas Storms, Hail & Creek-Side Flooding

Heavy spring storms and large hail can compromise roofing and flashing, and properties along Spring Creek, Rowlett Creek and near the Haggard Farm development area take on surface runoff during flash flooding events. Wet ceiling drywall and attic insulation require a different drying approach than floor-level water. See ceiling and drywall water damage and flood damage restoration.

Bathroom Overflows & Hidden Long-Term Moisture

A running toilet flapper, a failed angle-stop valve under a vanity, or a cracked shower pan can soak subfloors and wall framing for weeks before a stain appears on the surface. See toilet and bathroom overflow cleanup and our professional moisture mapping inspection if you suspect a hidden leak in your Plano home.

Our Process

How Our Water Damage Restoration Process Works

Our drying process follows the IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration. In plain terms, we measure everything, we don't guess.

01

Inspection

Source stopped, water category and class identified, every affected material moisture-mapped with FLIR thermal cameras and Protimeter meters.

Why it matters: Determines what can be saved and what must be removed.

02

Extraction

Standing and trapped water removed from floors, carpet, pad and wall cavities using truck-mounted and portable extractors.

Why it matters: Extraction removes water 1,200x faster than evaporation alone.

03

Controlled Demolition

Unsalvageable materials, wet carpet pad, swollen baseboards, saturated lower drywall, removed and disposed.

Why it matters: Prevents mold colonization, odor and hidden structural rot.

04

Structural Drying

LGR dehumidifiers, high-velocity air movers, wall-cavity drying systems and floor mat systems placed and balanced.

Why it matters: Dries the structure itself, framing, slab, subfloor, not just the air.

05

Daily Monitoring

Moisture readings taken and logged every day until every material hits its documented dry standard.

Why it matters: Proves the job is truly finished, your adjuster can verify every reading.

06

Restoration

Drywall, texture, paint, flooring, baseboards, trim and cabinets rebuilt to pre-loss condition.

Why it matters: One company from emergency call to final walkthrough.

Most residential water losses dry in three to five days once drying equipment is placed, depending on materials affected and how long the water sat before extraction. Concrete slabs, hardwood floors and multi-story losses can take longer, we tell you the timeline up front so there are no surprises.

Who should I call first when my house has water damage from a pipe leak? +

How fast does mold grow after water damage in North Texas? +

Mold can begin colonizing wet drywall, wood framing and carpet pad within 24 to 48 hours, and Collin County's humidity accelerates that timeline during spring and summer. Professional extraction and structural drying within the first 24 hours is the most effective mold prevention there is. See our mold remediation page.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage restoration in Plano, TX? +

Most homeowner policies in Texas cover sudden and accidental water damage, a burst supply line, a failed water heater, an overflowing appliance. Gradual leaks, maintenance issues and exterior flooding are often handled differently under your policy. We write Xactimate estimates and provide photo documentation and daily moisture logs so your adjuster can process the claim efficiently.

Can you work directly with my insurance adjuster? +

Yes. We document the loss, write the estimate in Xactimate, and communicate directly with your adjuster at State Farm, Allstate, USAA, Farmers, Liberty Mutual, Travelers or any carrier. We walk you through how payment works on your specific claim before major work begins.

Do I need to leave my house during water damage restoration? +

Usually not. Most families stay home while drying equipment runs. If there is a sewage backup, active mold growth or major structural demolition, we recommend keeping people and pets out of the affected area until it's cleared.

How much does water damage restoration cost in Plano, TX? +

What is the difference between water mitigation and water restoration? +

Water mitigation is the emergency phase, stopping the source, extracting water, removing unsalvageable materials and placing drying equipment to prevent further damage. Water restoration is the full process including mitigation, structural drying to verified dry standards, and reconstruction of everything that was removed, drywall, flooring, paint, trim and cabinets.
