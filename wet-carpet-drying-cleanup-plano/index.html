Quick Answer

How Long Can Carpet Stay Wet Before Mold Grows?

Mold can start growing in wet carpet and pad within 24 to 48 hours. In humid Plano homes, fast extraction and drying of the carpet and slab is the best way to prevent it.

Extraction Method

How Professional Carpet Extraction Works

Truck-Mounted Extraction

Our truck-mounted extractors connect heated vacuum lines from the truck to your home. The heated vacuum pulls water from carpet fibers and pad simultaneously at hundreds of gallons per hour, 50 to 100 times faster than a rental carpet cleaner or shop vac.

Weighted Extraction

Weighted extraction tools are flat metal heads that press down on the carpet, compressing the pad and forcing trapped water upward into the vacuum. A single pass with a weighted extractor can remove more water from carpet pad than 20 passes with a standard wand. On clean-water losses caught early, weighted extraction can save carpet pad that would otherwise need replacement, saving $2 to $4 per square foot in materials and labor.

Why It Matters

Why the Pad Usually Comes Out

Carpet pad is basically foam built to hold air, and it holds water just as well. On a concrete slab, which most Plano homes have, a wet pad traps water against the concrete where it can't evaporate.

When we pull back the wet carpet, remove the padding and dry the slab, a few things happen:

  • The slab can dry instead of staying wet under the pad
  • We can sanitize the slab and tack strip
  • Odor sources are removed
  • The carpet itself dries faster

New pad goes in once the slab is dry, and the carpet is reinstalled, re-stretched and seamed.

Carpet Float and Air Mover Drying

After extraction, carpet is floated, lifted slightly off the pad with small blocks, and air movers direct airflow between the carpet and pad to accelerate evaporation of remaining moisture. LGR dehumidifiers capture that moisture from the air.

Category 3 Water Means Carpet Disposal. If sewage, a toilet backup with waste, or outside floodwater touched the carpet, both carpet and pad are removed and disposed of. There is no reliable way to clean pathogens out of carpet fibers and pad. The slab is then disinfected.
Odor Control

Wet Carpet Smell and Mildew

If your soaked carpet smells like mildew after a leak, moisture is still trapped below. The smell comes from bacteria and mold growing in the wet pad and backing. Surface deodorizers won't fix it.

Our process:

  1. Extract remaining water
  2. Remove wet pad
  3. Treat the slab and carpet backing with an antimicrobial
  4. Dry carpet and subfloor with dehumidifiers and air movers
  5. Apply odor treatment once everything is dry
Prevention

Preventing Browning and Delamination

Wet carpet can develop brown stains as it dries, called browning, as minerals and fibers in the backing wick to the surface. Carpet left wet too long can also delaminate, meaning the backing separates from the fibers. Fast extraction and controlled drying help prevent both, and anti-browning treatment can be applied when needed.

Detail Work

Tack Strip Inspection

Tack strips along the walls hold your carpet in place. When they get wet, they can rust or rot. We check them during drying and replace any that are damaged before reinstalling.

DIY Warning

Why DIY Carpet Drying Fails

The mistake most Plano homeowners make is renting a carpet cleaner or using a shop vac, running box fans and opening windows, and assuming the carpet is dry when the surface feels dry. Here's why that doesn't work:

  • Shop vacs extract surface water only. They can't create the vacuum pressure needed to compress pad and extract trapped water.
  • Box fans dry the carpet face. The pad underneath, which holds 90% of the water, stays wet.
  • Open windows in North Texas humidity actually slow drying from April through October, when outdoor humidity exceeds 60%. You're introducing more moisture than you're removing.
  • Rental carpet cleaners are designed for cleaning, not extraction. They put water in and take some back out, they don't have the vacuum power for flood extraction.

Result: The carpet surface feels dry in 24 hours. The pad underneath stays wet for days. Mold starts growing on the pad and the bottom of the carpet within 48 hours. By the time you smell it, the entire carpet and pad need replacement, plus antimicrobial treatment of the subfloor.

FAQ

Wet Carpet FAQs

How long does wet carpet take to dry professionally? +

With professional truck-mounted extraction followed by air mover and LGR dehumidifier drying, most carpet dries in 1 to 2 days. Without professional extraction, carpet pad can stay wet for a week or more, long enough for mold to colonize. Call (214) 833-4785.

Does wet carpet always need to be replaced? +

No. Carpet soaked with Category 1 (clean) water and professionally extracted within 48 hours can almost always be saved. Pad can often be saved if weighted extraction happens within 24 hours. Category 2 water requires pad replacement but carpet can usually be saved with professional cleaning and antimicrobial treatment. Category 3 (sewage or flood water) requires full carpet and pad replacement. Call (214) 833-4785.

My carpet smells musty after it got wet, is that mold? +

A musty or sour smell from carpet that got wet is almost always mold or bacteria growing in the pad. The pad needs to come up, the carpet needs professional cleaning and antimicrobial treatment, and the subfloor needs inspection. Don't mask the smell with deodorizer, that doesn't address the contamination. Call (214) 833-4785. See mold remediation.

Does insurance cover wet carpet replacement? +

Insurance covers carpet replacement when the water damage is from a covered event (burst pipe, appliance failure, water heater failure) and the carpet is beyond saving. If professional extraction can save the carpet and pad, insurance covers the extraction and drying cost. We assess on-site and document the recommendation in Xactimate for your adjuster.
