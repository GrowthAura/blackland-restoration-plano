What Is Water Mitigation and Why Does Plano, TX Need It Fast?

Water mitigation is the emergency first response to a water loss, stopping the water source, extracting standing water, removing materials that can't be saved, and placing drying equipment to prevent further structural damage and mold growth. In Plano's North Texas climate where humidity runs high from April through October, mold can colonize wet drywall and framing within 24 to 48 hours. Calling (214) 833-4785 within the first hour of discovering water damage can cut your total repair cost by thousands and prevent a mitigation-only job from becoming a mitigation-plus-mold-remediation job.

The Difference

Water Mitigation vs. Water Restoration: What's the Difference?

Mitigation is the emergency phase: stop the source, extract water, remove unsalvageable materials, place drying equipment. The goal is to prevent further damage. Mitigation typically happens in the first 24 to 48 hours.

Restoration is the full process: mitigation, plus structural drying to verified dry standards (usually 3 to 5 days), plus reconstruction of everything that was removed, drywall, texture, paint, flooring, baseboards, trim, cabinets.

Most insurance policies cover mitigation as a separate line item because it reduces the overall claim cost. Delaying mitigation increases both, the damage and the claim.

Our Process

What Happens During Emergency Water Mitigation

01

Source Identification and Shutoff

Before we touch a single piece of equipment, we find where the water is coming from and stop it. Burst supply line? We shut the main valve. Overflowing appliance? We isolate the fixture. Slab leak? We help you coordinate with your plumber while we start protecting the structure. Active roof leak during a storm? We tarp and divert while setting up interior extraction.

02

Safety Assessment

We check for electrical hazards near wet areas, identify whether ceiling drywall is sagging and at risk of collapse, and classify the water category: Category 1 (Clean): Broken supply line, water heater tank failure, faucet leak; Category 2 (Gray): Washing machine overflow, dishwasher discharge, HVAC condensate; Category 3 (Black): Sewage backup, storm flooding, any water that contacted soil or waste. The category determines PPE requirements, which materials must be removed, and whether antimicrobial treatment is needed.

03

Emergency Water Extraction

Truck-mounted extractors pull hundreds of gallons per hour from carpet, pad, tile and hard surfaces. Weighted extraction tools press water out of carpet and pad without pulling them up, saving carpet that would otherwise be replaced. Submersible pumps handle standing water in garages, basements and mechanical rooms. Every gallon removed by extraction is a gallon that doesn't have to be evaporated by dehumidifiers over days.

04

Controlled Demolition

Wet carpet pad comes up, pad holds water like a sponge and cannot be dried in place. Baseboards are pulled so wall cavities can be accessed for drying. If drywall is saturated above the 2-foot line or is Category 3 contaminated, it's cut out to expose the framing behind it. We cut only what needs to go. Every piece removed is photographed and documented for your insurance claim.

05

Drying Equipment Placement

LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers are placed to control humidity in the drying chamber. High-velocity air movers direct airflow across wet surfaces and into wall cavities. Wall-cavity drying systems inject dry air directly behind drywall. Hardwood floor mat systems apply vacuum and heat to pull moisture through the wood without cupping. Equipment count and placement are calculated based on the IICRC S500 standard, not guesswork.

06

Insurance Documentation Starts Immediately

Before we move or remove anything, we photograph every affected area. Moisture readings from FLIR thermal cameras and Protimeter meters are recorded and will be logged daily until dry standards are met. The initial scope is written in Xactimate so your adjuster at State Farm, Allstate, USAA, Farmers or any carrier has documentation in the format they expect from the first day.

Common Scenarios

Common Plano Water Mitigation Scenarios

Burst Pipe in the Attic: Multi-Room Ceiling Damage

Homes in Hunters Glen, Deerfield, Canyon Creek and Chase Oaks with attic-mounted water heaters and HVAC units are especially vulnerable. When a supply line or tank fails in the attic, water travels through insulation and ceiling joists into multiple rooms below. Mitigation includes ceiling drywall removal, insulation pull, and drying of the attic deck, joists, wall cavities and flooring on the floor below. See burst pipe water damage.

Slab Leak: Slow Spread Under Flooring

Blackland Prairie clay soil shifts under Plano foundations and stresses copper supply lines beneath the concrete slab. A slab leak can run for days or weeks before the first damp baseboard appears. Mitigation for a slab leak includes moisture mapping the full extent of spread, removing affected flooring and pad, and placing LGR dehumidifiers and floor-directed air movers to dry the slab surface. See slab leak water damage.

Appliance Overflow: Kitchen and Laundry Floods

A washing machine hose failure, dishwasher drain backup, or refrigerator supply line crack can put 50 to 200 gallons on the floor in minutes. In two-story Plano homes, the water often runs through the subfloor to the ceiling below. Mitigation includes extracting from both levels, pulling baseboards, and drying wall cavities and subfloor from both sides. See appliance leak water damage.

Sewage Backup: Category 3 Emergency

A sewer main backup or toilet overflow with sewage is Category 3 contamination. Mitigation requires PPE, removal of all porous materials contacted by sewage (carpet, pad, lower drywall, insulation), EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment, and HEPA air filtration. No amount of drying makes Category 3 materials safe, they must come out. See sewage cleanup.

Preventing Secondary Mold Damage in Humid Texas Homes

Plano summers are humid, and that moisture in the air slows evaporation and keeps wet materials wet longer. Emergency water mitigation to stop mold growth works by lowering humidity inside the affected rooms below the levels mold needs. That is why we use commercial dehumidifiers instead of household units, and why we keep monitoring until readings confirm the structure is dry rather than just dry to the touch.

If mold has already started, our mold remediation team can step in under a separate containment plan.

FAQ

Water Mitigation FAQs

Should I call a plumber or a water mitigation company first? +

Call us at (214) 833-4785 first. We can help you shut off the water source immediately and start extraction while coordinating with your plumber. The pipe repair can happen alongside extraction, but every hour you wait for a plumber before calling mitigation, the water is spreading and the damage is growing.

Is water mitigation covered by homeowners insurance in Plano? +

Most policies cover mitigation as a separate line item for sudden and accidental water losses, burst pipes, failed water heaters, appliance overflows. Insurance companies actually prefer quick mitigation because it reduces the total claim cost. We document everything in Xactimate from day one. Call (214) 833-4785.

Can I do water mitigation myself with fans and a wet vac? +

A shop vac and box fans dry the surface, but they don't dry wall cavities, the underside of subfloor, framing inside walls, or the top of a concrete slab. Professional LGR dehumidifiers, calculated air mover placement, and daily moisture monitoring are what the IICRC S500 standard requires, and what your insurance adjuster expects to see documented in the claim. DIY drying is the number one cause of mold growth after a water loss.

What is the difference between water mitigation and mold remediation? +

Water mitigation prevents mold by drying materials before mold can grow, typically within the first 24 to 48 hours. Mold remediation removes mold that has already colonized wet materials. If mitigation starts fast enough, remediation isn't needed. That's why the first call matters more than any other step.
