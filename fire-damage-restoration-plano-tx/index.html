Quick Answer

What Should I Do After a Fire in My Plano Home?

After the fire department clears you to approach the property, do not enter until a structural assessment confirms it's safe. Do not turn on the HVAC system, it will spread soot and smoke residue throughout every room connected to the ductwork, including rooms the fire didn't reach. Do not attempt to clean soot yourself, wiping soot into porous surfaces makes it permanent. Call (214) 833-4785, our crew secures the property, assesses structural safety, and begins smoke and water damage mitigation the same day.

Dual Damage

Fire Damage Is Also Water Damage

Most homeowners don't realize that firefighting water causes as much or more damage than the fire itself in many residential fires. A kitchen fire contained to one room may result in water damage to three or four rooms from fire hoses and sprinkler discharge. That water is now sitting in your walls, under your flooring, soaking your subfloor and insulation, and without professional extraction and drying, it will grow mold within 48 hours.

Our restoration addresses both the fire/smoke damage and the water damage from firefighting simultaneously. See water mitigation and structural drying.

Soot Science

Types of Fire & Smoke Damage

Protein Residue (Kitchen Fires)

Kitchen fires, grease fires, oven fires, stovetop fires, produce protein-based smoke that is nearly invisible on surfaces but creates an extremely strong, pungent odor. Protein residue discolors paint and varnish when heated (by sunlight, by the HVAC system) and is one of the hardest smoke odors to eliminate.

Synthetic Residue (Electrical, Plastic, Furnishing Fires)

When synthetic materials burn, wiring insulation, plastic components, foam cushions, carpet, they produce thick, black, sticky soot that smears when touched. Synthetic soot is acidic and corrosive: it etches metal, permanently stains marble and grout, discolors painted surfaces, and pits chrome and brass fixtures within 24 to 72 hours if not cleaned.

Wood and Paper Smoke

Structure fires that burn framing, furniture and paper produce dry, powdery soot that covers surfaces in a fine gray-black layer. This soot is easier to clean than synthetic but penetrates deeply into porous materials and textiles.

Complex Residue (Mixed Fuel)

Most house fires burn a combination of natural and synthetic materials, producing complex residue that requires multiple cleaning methods and multiple passes. A single room fire can produce protein, synthetic and wood smoke residue simultaneously.

Odor Removal

Smoke Odor Removal

Smoke odor hides in drywall, insulation, carpet, upholstery and HVAC ducts. Cleaning the surfaces is step one. Then we treat what's left:

  • HEPA air scrubbers to remove airborne particles
  • Thermal fogging that penetrates the same way smoke did
  • Hydroxyl generators that break down odor molecules and can be used with people present
  • Ozone treatment in unoccupied spaces where appropriate
  • Sealing surfaces where odor can't be fully removed

Thermal fogging and ozone treatment for smoke smell are used together with cleaning, never as a replacement for it.

Air Quality

HVAC and Duct Cleaning After a Fire

Your HVAC system pulls smoke and soot through the whole house. If it ran during or after the fire, ducts and the air handler likely need cleaning and the system should not be run until it's been checked.

Kitchens

Kitchen Fire Restoration

Kitchen fires are the most common residential fire. Even small stovetop fires leave protein smoke residue across cabinets, walls and ceilings, along with strong odor. Kitchen fire restoration usually includes cabinet cleaning or replacement, ceiling and wall cleaning, sealing and repainting, and appliance replacement coordination.

Structure

Charred Framing and Structural Repair

Burned studs and attic trusses may be cleaned and sealed if structurally sound, or replaced if not. Soda or dry-ice blasting can remove char and soot from framing. Then our reconstruction team handles framing, insulation, drywall, finishes and coordination with licensed electricians and plumbers.

Belongings

Protecting Your Belongings

Clothes, furniture, electronics, documents and keepsakes can often be cleaned and deodorized. Our contents pack-out service inventories, removes, cleans and stores belongings while your home is restored.

Restoration Process

Fire & Smoke Damage Restoration Process

01

Emergency Board-Up and Securing

Windows, doors and roof openings left by the fire or fire department access are boarded up to secure the property from weather, animals and unauthorized entry. Tarps are placed over roof damage to prevent rain from adding to the water damage.

02

Water Extraction and Drying

Firefighting water is extracted from carpets, pads, walls, subfloor and any standing-water areas. LGR dehumidifiers and air movers dry the structure. This runs simultaneously with smoke damage work. See water extraction.

03

Soot and Smoke Residue Removal

Every affected surface is cleaned using the method matched to the soot type: dry sponge (chemical sponge) for initial removal to prevent smearing, then wet cleaning with specialized smoke residue cleaners. HEPA vacuuming removes particulate from all surfaces including textiles, upholstery and HVAC ductwork.

04

Smoke Odor Elimination

Surface cleaning alone doesn't eliminate smoke odor, molecules penetrate drywall, wood, concrete and soft goods. Professional odor elimination uses thermal fogging (penetrates the same spaces smoke penetrated) and hydroxyl generators (break down odor molecules at the molecular level). These are not air fresheners, they destroy the odor source.

05

Contents Cleaning and Pack-Out

Salvageable contents, furniture, clothing, electronics, documents, artwork, are inventoried, packed out to a climate-controlled facility, and professionally cleaned using methods matched to each material (ultrasonic cleaning for hard goods, ozone chambers for soft goods, freeze-drying for documents). See contents pack-out.

06

Structural Assessment and Demo

Fire-damaged structural members, framing, joists, rafters, subfloor, are assessed for structural integrity. Charred wood that has lost structural capacity is removed. Smoke-contaminated drywall, insulation and materials that can't be cleaned to pre-loss condition are removed.

07

Reconstruction

Complete rebuild of damaged structure, framing, drywall, roofing, flooring, paint, trim, fixtures, cabinets, countertops. See water damage reconstruction.

FAQ

Fire Damage FAQs

How long does fire damage restoration take? +

A small kitchen fire with moderate smoke damage may take 2 to 4 weeks for cleaning and repairs. A significant structure fire requiring major demolition and reconstruction can take 3 to 6 months. We give you a detailed timeline after the initial assessment and update it as work progresses. Call (214) 833-4785.

Does homeowners insurance cover fire damage? +

Yes, fire is a named peril covered by virtually every Texas homeowner policy. Coverage typically includes structural damage, contents (personal property), additional living expenses (hotel, food, temporary housing while your home is being restored), and debris removal. We document the entire loss in Xactimate and work directly with your adjuster at State Farm, Allstate, USAA, Farmers, Liberty Mutual, Travelers or any carrier. Call (214) 833-4785.

Can smoke-damaged clothes and furniture be saved? +

Many items can be professionally restored, clothing through specialized laundering and ozone treatment, hard furniture through soot removal and refinishing, electronics through ultrasonic cleaning. Heavily charred, melted or structurally compromised items need replacement. Our contents team assesses each item and documents what's salvageable vs. what needs replacement for your insurance claim. Call (214) 833-4785.

My house smells like smoke weeks after the fire, why? +

Surface cleaning wasn't enough. Smoke molecules penetrate drywall, wood framing, concrete, insulation and soft goods at a molecular level. Painting over smoke-damaged surfaces traps the odor underneath, it comes back when temperatures rise. Professional thermal fogging and hydroxyl treatment are needed to break down the odor molecules inside the materials. Call (214) 833-4785.

Should I turn on my HVAC after a fire? +

No, absolutely not until the ductwork has been inspected and cleaned. The HVAC system pulls air from every room and distributes it throughout the house. Turning it on after a fire spreads soot, ash and smoke residue into every room on the system, including rooms the fire never touched. This turns a one-room cleanup into a whole-house job. Call (214) 833-4785 before turning on any building system.
