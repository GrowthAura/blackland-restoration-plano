Health Risks

Health Risks of a Sewer Backup

Sewage is classified as Category 3, or black water, and can contain:

  • Bacteria such as E. coli and Salmonella
  • Viruses, including hepatitis A
  • Parasites and fungi
  • Contaminated sludge that soaks into porous materials

That's why sewer backup restoration in Plano TX isn't a mop-and-bleach job. Porous materials that absorb sewage can't be reliably cleaned, and fumes and residue can linger long after the water is gone.

Root Causes

What Causes Sewage Backups in Plano, TX Homes?

Sewage backups in Plano come from several sources, and knowing the cause determines the scope of cleanup:

City Sewer Main Overload During Heavy Rain

Plano's sanitary sewer system is separate from storm drains, but during extreme rainfall events, groundwater infiltration and illegal stormwater connections can overwhelm the system. When the city main backs up, sewage comes up through the lowest drain in your home, usually a ground-floor toilet, bathtub, or floor drain.

Private Sewer Line Failures

The sewer lateral connecting your home to the city main is your responsibility. Tree roots from mature oaks, pecans and crepe myrtles common in Willow Bend, Preston Meadow, Haggard Park and established East Plano neighborhoods penetrate pipe joints and create blockages. Orangeburg (bituminous fiber) pipe used in some 1960s-1970s homes collapses over time. Even newer PVC lines can be crushed by clay soil movement or separated at joints.

Cast Iron Drain Pipe Deterioration

Homes built before the late 1980s in Plano and neighboring Richardson often have cast iron waste pipes under the slab. Cast iron corrodes from the inside over decades, developing scale buildup that catches solids and eventually cracks or collapses. A failed cast iron drain under a slab produces a sewage leak that can run for weeks before it surfaces, and the contamination spreads through the slab pores.

Toilet Overflow with Sewage

A clogged toilet that overflows while connected to a sewer line introduces Category 3 water to bathroom floors. If the overflow contacts carpet, baseboard, vanity cabinet or extends into adjoining rooms, professional extraction and sanitization are needed. See toilet overflow cleanup.

Our Process

Sewage Cleanup Process: Category 3 Protocol

01

Containment

We seal off the affected area to prevent cross-contamination to the rest of the home. Negative air machines with HEPA filters create negative pressure in the contaminated zone so airborne pathogens don't spread through the HVAC system.

02

PPE and Extraction

Our technicians wear full PPE, Tyvek suits, N95 or P100 respirators, rubber boots, chemical-resistant gloves. Sewage is extracted with dedicated equipment that is decontaminated after every Category 3 job.

03

Porous Material Removal

Everything porous that sewage contacted must come out, no exceptions under IICRC S500. That includes carpet, pad, lower drywall (cut 24 inches above the visible contamination line), baseboard, insulation, particleboard, MDF and any upholstered furniture or mattresses. Every removed item is photographed for your insurance documentation.

04

Antimicrobial Treatment

All remaining structural surfaces, concrete slab, framing, upper drywall, subfloor, receive two rounds of EPA-registered antimicrobial treatment: one immediately after demolition and one after drying. This kills bacteria, viruses and fungi on non-porous surfaces.

05

HEPA Air Scrubbing

HEPA-filtered negative air machines run continuously throughout demolition and drying to capture airborne particulates, bacterial aerosols and mold spores. See mold remediation.

06

Structural Drying

LGR dehumidifiers and air movers dry the exposed structure to verified dry standards. Daily moisture readings are logged until every material hits its target. Sewage-affected structures often require longer drying times because contaminated drywall is cut higher, exposing more cavity area.

07

Reconstruction

Once dry and cleared, we rebuild everything that was removed: drywall, texture, paint, flooring, baseboards, vanity cabinets, trim. See water damage reconstruction.

Is Sewage Cleanup Covered by Insurance in Plano?

Most homeowner policies cover sewage backup damage if you have the sewer and drain backup endorsement (also called Service Line coverage) on your policy. This endorsement is optional and typically adds $5,000 to $25,000 in coverage. Check your policy declarations page or call your agent at State Farm, Allstate, USAA, Farmers or your carrier.

Without the endorsement, a sewage backup may not be covered, and you would be responsible for cleanup costs out of pocket. FAQ

Sewage Cleanup FAQs

Can I clean up a small sewage overflow myself? +

If sewage contacted only hard, non-porous surfaces (tile floor, sealed concrete) and was cleaned and disinfected with a bleach solution within one hour, a small overflow may not require professional remediation. However, if sewage contacted carpet, pad, drywall, baseboard, grout, wood flooring or any porous surface, or if the overflow was more than a few square feet, professional Category 3 cleanup is necessary. When in doubt, call (214) 833-4785 for an assessment.

How long does sewage cleanup take? +

Depending on the affected area, sewage cleanup typically takes 5 to 10 days total: 1 to 2 days for extraction, demolition and antimicrobial treatment; 3 to 5 days for structural drying; then reconstruction time varies based on scope. We give you the full timeline after the initial assessment. Call (214) 833-4785.

Is sewage cleanup dangerous without PPE? +

Yes. Sewage contains E. coli, Salmonella, Hepatitis A, Cryptosporidium, Giardia and other pathogens. Exposure through skin contact, inhalation of aerosols, or ingestion can cause serious illness. IICRC standards require full PPE for any technician working in a Category 3 environment. Do not enter a sewage-affected area without proper protection. Call (214) 833-4785.

My home smells like sewage but I don't see any water, do I still need cleanup? +

A sewage odor without visible water often indicates a slow leak from a cracked or separated drain pipe under the slab. The contamination may be migrating through slab pores or seeping through cracks in the concrete. A moisture mapping inspection with FLIR thermal imaging can locate the source without demolishing your floors. Call (214) 833-4785.
