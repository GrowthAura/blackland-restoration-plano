The Attic Risk

Why Are Water Heater and AC Leaks So Damaging in Plano?

Most cities install water heaters in the garage or a ground-floor utility closet. Plano builders, especially in homes built from the 1990s through today in Deerfield, Hunters Glen, Canyon Creek, Kings Ridge, Chase Oaks, Preston Meadow and most of 75024, 75025 and 75093, put tank water heaters in the attic to save floor space. Many also put the air handler and condensate system in the attic.

When a 40-to-50-gallon water heater tank fails in the attic, all that water plus the incoming supply line flow drops through attic insulation, soaks ceiling joists, saturates ceiling drywall, and cascades to every floor below. An AC condensate overflow does the same, less volume, but the same downward path through the structure.

Leak Types

Types of Water Heater & AC Leaks

Tank Water Heater Failure

Tank water heaters have a typical lifespan of 8 to 12 years. As the steel tank corrodes from the inside, the bottom weakens and eventually fails. When it goes, 40 to 50 gallons release at once, plus the supply line continues feeding water until someone shuts it off. If the water heater is in the attic and no one is home, hundreds of gallons can flow through the house before the leak is discovered.

Signs of a failing water heater: Rust-colored water from hot taps, moisture or rust at the base of the tank, water in the drain pan, tank age over 8 years, popping or rumbling sounds during heating cycles.

AC Condensate Overflow

Air conditioning systems produce condensation as they cool air. That moisture drains through a condensate line, typically PVC, to the outside of the house or to a drain. When the condensate line clogs with algae, mold or debris (extremely common in North Texas humidity from May through October), water backs up into the overflow pan. If the overflow pan's secondary drain is also clogged or improperly connected, water overflows onto the ceiling below.

Prevention tip: Pour a cup of white vinegar down the condensate drain line every month during cooling season. Have the line professionally flushed during your spring HVAC maintenance.

Tankless Water Heater Leak

Tankless water heaters rarely produce catastrophic floods like tank failures, but they can develop leaks at supply connections, heat exchanger joints, and pressure relief valves. The slow, steady drip damages surrounding materials over time.

AC Leaks

AC Leak Water Damage in Plano

Texas summers mean air conditioners run hard for months, and they produce a lot of condensation. When the primary drain line clogs, water fills the pan. If the secondary pan is rusted or the float switch doesn't shut the unit off, that water goes into the ceiling.

Common signs of AC leak water damage:

  • Water dripping from an AC vent in the ceiling
  • Brown rings on the ceiling near the attic access or air handler
  • Musty smell when the AC runs
  • Bubbling paint on the ceiling

If water is dripping through a vent, turn the system off at the thermostat and call us. We'll dry the ceiling and attic, and your HVAC technician can clear the line.

Full Drying

Drying Attic Decking, Joists and Hallway Ceilings

Attic losses need drying from above and below. We remove saturated insulation, dry the ceiling joists and drywall, and dry the hallway or room ceiling underneath. If the ceiling has to come down, we cut it in a controlled way and replace it during the rebuild.

Replacing Wet Attic Insulation

Wet insulation loses much of its R-value, compresses and holds moisture against the ceiling drywall. Soaked blown-in insulation is removed with commercial vacuums, and new insulation is installed once the attic is dry.

Preventing Mold in the Attic and Ductwork

Attics are hot and humid in Plano summers. Add water and mold can grow quickly on drywall paper, wood and insulation. We apply antimicrobial treatment, verify dryness with meters and check around ducts and the air handler platform. If mold is already visible, our mold remediation team handles removal.

Insurance note

Insurance and Water Heater Leaks. The water damage from a sudden water heater failure is often covered by homeowners insurance. The water heater itself typically isn't. Slow leaks from a pan that's been rusting for months may be treated differently. We provide photos, moisture readings and an itemized estimate for your adjuster.

Prevention

Prevention Tips for Plano Homeowners

  • Have your water heater flushed and inspected yearly
  • Replace tank water heaters around the 10 to 12 year mark
  • Add a pan with a drain line and a leak sensor under attic units
  • Have your HVAC technician clear the condensate line each spring
  • Make sure a float switch is installed on the secondary drain pan

FAQ

Water Heater & AC Leak FAQs

My water heater is in the attic, how do I know if it's about to fail? +

Check the manufacture date on the label, tanks over 8 years old are approaching end of life. Look for rust at the base, moisture in the drain pan, rust-colored hot water, or popping/rumbling sounds. If you see any of these signs, have a plumber inspect it before it fails catastrophically. If it's already leaking, call (214) 833-4785 immediately.

My AC is dripping through the ceiling, is this an emergency? +

Yes. An AC condensate overflow in the attic sends water through insulation and ceiling joists the same way a water heater failure does. Turn off the HVAC system to stop the flow, then call (214) 833-4785. The longer the condensate runs, the more insulation and ceiling drywall needs to be replaced.

Does insurance cover water heater failure in the attic? +

Yes, a failed water heater tank is sudden and accidental water damage covered by most Texas homeowner policies. The water damage to ceilings, walls, insulation, flooring and contents is covered. The water heater replacement itself may not be. We document the entire loss in Xactimate for your adjuster at State Farm, Allstate, USAA, Farmers or any carrier. Call (214) 833-4785.

How do I prevent AC condensate overflow? +

Monthly maintenance during cooling season: pour a cup of white vinegar down the condensate drain line to kill algae buildup. Have your HVAC technician flush the line and check the overflow pan and secondary drain during spring maintenance. Consider installing a condensate line safety switch that shuts off the AC if the line backs up, it costs about $20 and prevents thousands in water damage.
